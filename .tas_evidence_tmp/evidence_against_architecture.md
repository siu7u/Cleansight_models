# Evidence for an "it's a data/label/observation problem, not an architecture problem" section

All items below were verified by fetching the page myself. Fetched artifacts: `.tas_evidence_tmp/cache/` (arXiv abs pages), `.tas_evidence_tmp/ft/` (full-text HTML→txt).

## 1. Annotation ambiguity / inter-annotator agreement

- **Proposal and multicentric validation of a laparoscopic Roux-en-Y gastric bypass surgery ontology** — Surgical Endoscopy, 2023. Fetched: https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&retmode=xml&id=36289088 (DOI 10.1007/s00464-022-09745-2)
  Two board-certified surgeons labelled 40+70 LRYGB videos with a 12-phase / 46-step ontology. Cohen's kappa was **95.9 ± 4.3% for phases but 80.8 ± 10.0% for steps**; inter-rater accuracy 96.8/85.4% (phases/steps) on StraBypass40 and 94.9/76.1% on BernBypass70; intra-rater accuracy 98.4% for phases vs 88.1% for steps. Agreement degrades sharply as labels get finer, with the same experts and an explicit manual. **Strong** — this is exactly a whole-segment class-identity reliability bound, and it falls fastest at fine granularity.
- **Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty video annotation: analysis of the learning curve of three annotators** — International Journal of Surgery, 2026. Fetched: same eutils endpoint, `id=42682465` (DOI 10.1097/JS9.0000000000005019)
  Three annotators, expert-written manual, 40 videos: pairwise Cohen's kappa only **0.44–0.58**. **Strong** — even coarse "phases/features" reach only moderate agreement across annotators with different expertise.
- **A systematic review of annotation for surgical process model analysis in minimally invasive surgery based on video** — Surgical Endoscopy, 2023. Fetched: eutils `id=37157035` (DOI 10.1007/s00464-023-10041-w)
  34 of 2806 screened articles qualified (31 = 88.2% phase/step/action recognition). Verdict quoted: "The process of annotation for surgical process model was lacking and poorly described, and description of the surgical procedures was highly variable"; "Surgical video annotation lacks a rigorous and reproducible framework." **Strong** — field-level evidence that the ground truth itself is under-specified.
- **Standardization of surgical gesture taxonomy: a SAGES Delphi consensus study** — Surgical Endoscopy, 2026. Fetched: eutils `id=42141188` (DOI 10.1007/s00464-026-12906-2)
  Expert panel reduced 270 literature terms to 10 clusters / 24 gestures / 46 sub-gestures; "video-based validation … identifying predictable ambiguities among semantically proximate actions (e.g., **cut vs seal; grasp vs clamp; dissect vs spread**)", and "Defining temporal boundaries for these gestures was identified as the next critical step." **Strong** — published expert consensus that specific fine-grained class pairs are not reliably separable, i.e. annotation ambiguity by construction.
- **Action classification for endoscopic pituitary adenoma resection: a consensus-based study** — IJCARS, 2026. Fetched: eutils `id=41964780`
  Independent annotator vs ontology: kappa 0.69–0.95 ("substantial to near-perfect"). **Contradicts / weakens** — coarse instrument–verb–target triplets *can* be annotated reproducibly, so ambiguity is granularity- and ontology-dependent, not universal.
- **Absent:** I found **no** inter-annotator-agreement study for TAS itself (Breakfast/50Salads/GTEA). arXiv `searchtype=all` queries `"temporal action segmentation" annotator agreement`, `action segmentation label noise robustness` (6 hits, none an agreement study), `annotation ambiguity surgical workflow` (1 irrelevant hit) returned nothing on point. Report this as a genuine gap.

## 2. Fine-grained / direction-dependent actions and observation informativeness

- **Temporal Action Segmentation: An Analysis of Modern Techniques** — Ding, Sener, Yao, IEEE TPAMI 2024 (DOI 10.1109/tpami.2023.3327284). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10352 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10352
  On input features: pre-computed features (IDT hand-crafted, I3D CNN) are the standard TAS input, but "as pointed out by [Choi et al. 2019; Huang et al. 2018], **pre-computed characteristics tend to favour static cues, e.g., scene components, within frames**", and "To the best of our knowledge, **no empirical research has compared utilizing pre-computed features to training TAS models from raw images end-to-end**." **Moderate-to-strong** — the community has never established that pre-computed/detection-derived features carry enough information, which is precisely the project's 144-dim YOLO-feature situation.
- **Cost-Sensitive Learning for Long-Tailed Temporal Action Segmentation** — Pang, Sener, Ramasubramanian, Yao; arXiv preprint 2025 (no Crossref record). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18358 and https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18358v1
  Diagnoses class-level bias and states tail actions are *under-learnt* "because the learning of tail is suppressed due to the temporal continuity of frame representation… **Distinctly separating two consecutive actions, one being head and the other tail, is challenging as they share similar frame representations**, especially at segment boundaries." **Strong** — a direct statement that different classes are not separable in the frame representation, i.e. a feature-informativeness ceiling.
- **HeiChole benchmark** — Medical Image Analysis, 2023. Fetched: eutils `id=36889206` (DOI 10.1016/j.media.2023.102770)
  33 laparoscopic cholecystectomies, 12 international teams, identical data. F1 was **23.9–67.7% for phase recognition (n=9)** and **38.5–63.8% for instrument presence (n=8)**, but only **21.8–23.3% for action recognition (n=5)**. **Strong** — when the object/tool signal is available (instrument presence) models do well; the moment the label requires the *action verb* rather than the object, every architecture collapses to ~22%. This is the closest published analogue to "insert vs withdraw is not in the detection features".
- **Disentangling Static and Dynamic Information for Reducing Static Bias in Action Recognition** — arXiv preprint 2025. Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23009
  "Action recognition models rely excessively on static cues rather than dynamic human motion"; the paper separates dynamic from static streams. **Weak-to-moderate** — establishes static bias exists as a measurable phenomenon, but is not about direction-dependent classes.
- **Absent / not found:** no paper I could verify that studies **insert-vs-withdraw-type direction-dependent** surgical actions, or that reports a negative result on direction discrimination from tool-presence features. I searched arXiv (`surgical action recognition insertion withdrawal direction`, `motion direction action recognition limitation` → 8 hits, none on point) and PubMed (`surgical instrument insertion withdrawal recognition` → 2 hits, both clinical case reports; `surgical action recognition motion direction instrument trajectory` → 0 hits). State this limitation explicitly rather than padding it.

## 3. How much data TAS / surgical phase models need

- **Learning from a tiny dataset of manual annotations: a teacher/student approach for surgical phase recognition** — arXiv preprint (v3, 2020; no Crossref venue match). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00033 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.00033
  Cholec80, CNN-biLSTM-CRF accuracy as a function of *number of annotated videos*: **40.1% (1 video) → 71.1% (3) → 76.2% (5) → 78.5% (10) → 84.1% (20) → 89.5% (80)**; average F1 went 21.1 → 55.3 → 65.8 → 69.7 → 75.8 → 82.5. Per-phase precision at 1 video was 5.5 ± 8.4. **Strong** — a clean published learning curve showing the dominant variable is the amount of dense annotation, not model design.
- **TAS survey (as above), Section V:** "Procedural video sequences feature enormous temporal redundancy in the supervisory signals… Such redundancy is been proven by the comparable performance of using timestamp supervision vs. fully-supervised setting." **Moderate** — labels are partly redundant, so adding *more videos* (not more label types) is the binding constraint.
- **Distill and Collect for Semi-Supervised Temporal Action Segmentation** — arXiv preprint 2022. Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01311
  States plainly that frame annotations "are very expensive and time-consuming… limits their performances when only limited annotated data is available", and shows unannotated in-domain video can recover performance "comparable to the full supervision". **Moderate** — unlabelled data substitutes for labels, again pointing at data rather than architecture.
- **Scale context (TAS survey, Table II):** 50Salads = 5.5 h / 50 videos / 0.9 K segments; Breakfast = 77 h / 1712 videos / 11 K segments. The project's ~30 K frames sits far below even 50Salads. **Moderate** — a published-scale argument that ~30 K frames is off the low end of the literature's operating range.
- **Absent:** I found no published learning curve for MS-TCN/MS-TCN++ on Breakfast/50Salads/GTEA as a function of training-set fraction. Any claim that "MS-TCN overfits at this data size" is **UNVERIFIED** in the literature I could fetch.

## 4. Is per-frame accuracy architecture-insensitive while structure is architecture-sensitive?

The same paper gives the cleanest controlled numbers, on identical features (I3D), splits and protocol — **Cost-Sensitive Learning… (arXiv 2503.18358 / html v1)**, Breakfast:

| Model family | per-class Acc | per-class F1@10 | global Acc | global Edit | global F1@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MS-TCN (TCN) | 49.1 | 48.1 | 67.7 | 66.6 | 63.2 |
| ASFormer (Transformer) | 52.3 | 57.9 | 72.4 | 74.5 | 75.5 |
| DiffAct (diffusion) | 56.2 | 63.3 | — | — | 75.5 (G_F1) |

Across the full span of three architecture families, relative gains are **per-class Acc +14.5%** and **per-class F1@10 +31.6%**; global Acc +6.9%, Edit +11.9%, F1@10 +19.5%. Segment-level metrics are roughly 2–3× more architecture-sensitive than frame accuracy in relative terms. **Moderate** — supports the direction of the project's claim, but note it **contradicts** the strong form: frame accuracy *does* move materially with architecture (49.1 → 56.2).
- **ASFormer** — BMVC 2021 (DOI 10.5244/c.35.49). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08568 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.08568
  Its own Table 7 (50Salads / GTEA / Breakfast, all with the same protocol): MS-TCN → ASFormer gives Acc 80.7→85.6, 76.3→79.7, 66.3→73.5, while Edit goes 67.9→79.6, 69.8→79.2, 61.7→75.0. **Contradicts** the strong claim on standard benchmarks: architecture family moved frame accuracy by 3–7 points there.
  Its Table 8 is the more informative control: with the *identical* ASRF refinement module, swapping the MS-TCN backbone for ASFormer gives only **+1.4 Acc** but **+2.6 Edit** and +1.9 F1@10 (84.5/79.3/84.9 → 85.9/81.9/86.8). **Moderate** — architecture contributes more to structure than to frame accuracy once the readout is fixed.

## 5. Are segment metrics dominated by easy/long classes?

- **TAS survey (IEEE TPAMI 2024), Section II-D**, fetched full text as above — the only explicit metric critique I could verify: "**The MoF metric can be problematic under dataset imbalance, i.e. if frequent and long action classes dominate.** The long-tailed nature of current datasets… implies that models with similar MoF scores may have large qualitative differences, suggesting that class-balanced metrics may be more appropriate **though this is currently not adopted in the literature**. Futhermore, MoF, as a per-frame calculation, **does not capture segment quality**; the score can be high even when the segments are fragmented." It also tabulates Imbalance Ratio: **GTEA 24, 50Salads 6, Breakfast 639, Assembly101 2604**, and notes Breakfast's head class 'fry pancake' is 639× more frequent than the tail class 'take butter'. **Strong.**
- **Cost-Sensitive Learning… (arXiv 2503.18358), Tables 2/3/8** give an exact quantification of the inflation: MS-TCN on Breakfast scores **67.7% global frame accuracy but only 49.1% per-class frame accuracy**; ASFormer **72.4% global vs 52.3% per-class** — a ~19–20 point gap caused purely by class frequency. **Strong** — this is the published justification for distrusting a headline MoF such as the project's 53.98.
- **Absent:** I could not verify any paper that specifically critiques **F1@IoU** for TAS (random-overlap or multi-threshold credit-assignment issues). Searches `action segmentation evaluation metric critique`, `evaluation of temporal action segmentation metrics` (26 hits), `temporal action localization evaluation metric limitations` (9 hits) produced no such paper. Any F1@IoU critique in the survey should be labelled as our own argument, not cited.

## 6. Class imbalance / extremely rare classes

- **Cost-Sensitive Learning… (arXiv 2503.18358)**: Table 1 splits Breakfast into **20 head (≥5·10⁴ frames) / 28 tail (≤5·10⁴)** classes, 50Salads 6/13, Assembly101 31/171. Table 3 group-wise results: MS-TCN Breakfast head Acc 65.1 vs **tail Acc 37.7**; Assembly101 head 33.9 vs **tail 4.7**. Figure 1 documents "low accuracy on tail actions with AsFormer" and slower tail convergence. **Strong.**
- Same paper, Table 2 — **directly relevant to the project's failed loss-weighting attempts**: adding standard imbalance remedies to MS-TCN/ASFormer/DiffAct changes per-class scores by ≈0, and often negatively (Breakfast MS-TCN: +CB +0.9/+0.7/+0.3, +LA +1.0/+1.1/+0.1, +Focal +0.2/−0.3/−1.2, +τ-norm −1.1/−1.0/−1.0). Only their transition-aware cost-sensitive scheme helps (+8.1/+8.1/+5.7). **Strong** — published confirmation that generic re-weighting does not fix rare/fine classes.
- **tiny-dataset surgical paper (arXiv 1812.00033)**: per-phase precision at 1 annotated video was 5.5 ± 8.4 (Phase 1) and average F1 5 ± 2.9, recovering only with more videos. **Moderate** — rare classes are the first casualty of small dense-labelled data.

## Synthesis (honest)

1. On annotation: the surgical-data-science community *has* measured this, and the measurements consistently show agreement degrading with granularity — 95.9% kappa on phases vs 80.8% on steps with the same two experts, and only 0.44–0.58 kappa between three annotators on a single procedure — while a systematic review concludes surgical video annotation "lacks a rigorous and reproducible framework."
2. That is the closest verifiable analogue to a 51.6% whole-segment majority-label error rate, but it is a *different* domain, and I could not find a single inter-annotator-agreement study for TAS itself, so claiming "our labels are ambiguous" from literature alone would overreach.
3. On features, the strongest verifiable support is indirect but consistent: the HeiChole benchmark collapses from 38.5–63.8% F1 on instrument presence to 21.8–23.3% F1 on action recognition across five independent teams, and the TAS survey states outright that pre-computed features "favour static cues" and that no one has ever tested whether they suffice.
4. On data, the one clean published learning curve (40.1% → 89.5% accuracy from 1 to 80 annotated surgical videos) plus the absence of any TAS learning-curve study means the honest framing is "nobody has shown 30 K frames is enough", not "30 K frames is provably too few".
5. On architecture, the literature partly **contradicts** the strong version of the claim — per-class accuracy does rise with architecture (49.1 → 52.3 → 56.2 across TCN/Transformer/diffusion) — but the *relative* gains concentrate 2–3× more in segment metrics, and the published per-class-vs-global accuracy gap (49.1 vs 67.7) independently confirms that our 53.98 MoF is an inflated quantity; the defensible section is therefore "architecture is a second-order lever here, and our headline metric is misleading", not "architecture does not matter".
