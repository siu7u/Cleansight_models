# Offline / full-sequence TAS architectures for CleanSight
### A verified literature survey — targeted at a 144-dim feature, tiny-data, whole-segment-identity ceiling

**Date:** 2026-09-27 · **Scope:** offline (full-sequence) temporal action segmentation (TAS) methods that could plausibly break a "whole-segment label confusion" ceiling, with emphasis on low-dimensional pre-extracted features and small datasets.

---

## 0. Headline verdict (read this first)

1. **Of the three possible levers — capacity, architecture/output-formulation, decoding — the literature says: capacity is not the lever, the other two both are.** Architecture *does* matter when it changes the **output formulation**: MS-TCN → DiffAct is a **+16.7 Edit** jump on 50Salads, and UVAST beats ASFormer on Edit (Breakfast 77.2 vs 75.0; 50Salads 83.9 vs 79.6) with **fewer** parameters (1.10M vs 1.13M). What does *not* work is adding width/depth within a frame-wise family — which is exactly what our in-house h256/deeper/plain-MS-TCN results already showed. Separately, the cheapest verified segment-metric gains come from **decoding-side** interventions that re-decide a segment's label or forbid inadmissible label sequences (S-NCM, constrained Viterbi, grammar parsing), two of which need **no retraining at all**. Practical reading: if we spend on architecture, spend it on changing what the model *outputs*, not on making it bigger.
2. **We can already falsify the "capacity/architecture" answer in-house.** Our own probes show the dominant error class is (a) insensitive to capacity and architecture (§6.1), and (b) for two of the four worst classes, **information-theoretically absent from the 144-dim features** (§6.2). No architecture can recover information that is not in the input.
3. **Therefore the ranked shortlist below is deliberately weighted toward cheap, falsifiable, decoding-side interventions**, with the one architecture (FACT) that models segment identity *by construction* placed highest among the retraining options.
4. **"Buy a bigger/better feature encoder" is now the best-refuted option on the table.** A controlled ICRA 2026 sweep over 14 encoders spanning 16.2× in parameters moves the average TAS metric by only 2.2–8.9 points, while removing one pipeline stage costs 15.8–40.6 (§4.10) — and larger checkpoints *underperform* smaller ones across all four VLM families.
5. **A cheaper feature-side experiment is still untried and is better motivated than any architecture.** Our 144-dim vector is *instantaneous*; the one published study that isolates this (Funke et al., MICCAI 2019, JIGSAWS) shows frame accuracy unchanged but **Edit 41.4 → 64.0** from adding spatio-temporal content alone. See §4.10.
6. **Honest expectation setting:** the mechanisms below can move `edit` / `insert recall` / over-segmentation, and can recover identity for classes where the *representation* still separates classes but the *classifier* is biased or miscalibrated. They **cannot** fix `flush`/`withdraw`/`sbc` if the in-house §6.2 diagnosis holds. Their main value is that they are cheap enough to settle the question empirically in days.

## 4. Deep dives on the specific items requested

### 4.1 ASFormer (BMVC 2021) — [arXiv 2110.08568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08568)
Dilated temporal convolutions replace the Transformer FFN, self-attention is restricted to a local window (w = 2^i), plus hierarchical representation and three cross-attention decoders. Reported: 50Salads F1 85.1/83.4/76.0, Edit 79.6, Acc 85.6; GTEA Edit 84.6; Breakfast Edit 75.0 (I3D 2048-d, stated explicitly); **1.134M params**; MIT code.
**Two findings directly relevant to us.** (1) The paper's own motivation is *"the lack of inductive biases with small training sets"* and it constrains the hypothesis space to help *"learn a proper target function with small training sets"*. (2) Its Table 3 ablation is the cleanest citable explanation of why our vanilla Transformer lost 24 edit points: swapping temporal convolution for MLP + positional encoding costs **25.5 F1@10 and 23.3 Edit**, while frame accuracy moves only ~3 points. The Transformer loss is a *known, quantified* inductive-bias effect, not a mysterious result.

### 4.2 UVAST (ECCV 2022) — [arXiv 2209.00638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00638)
Views TAS as seq2seq translation "mapping a sequence of video frames to a sequence of action segments"; auxiliary frame-wise encoder loss, a separate alignment decoder for implicit duration prediction, and Viterbi/constrained-k-medoids for timestamp supervision. `Edit` is one of its headline metrics (76.9 / 83.9 / 92.1 on Breakfast / 50Salads / GTEA without duration modelling).
**It names our failure mode, and it does so in its own abstract-adjacent text.** From the paper: *"the encoder performs frame-wise classification with **high localization performance, i.e., high frame-wise accuracy, but low discrimination performance**, i.e., over-segmentation with low Edit distance to the ground truth"*, attributed to *"the relatively small amount of training videos"*. That is a published, independent statement of exactly the phenomenon we observe (boundaries roughly right, segment identity wrong, small data).
**And it shows capacity is not the lever:** UVAST reaches better Edit than ASFormer with *fewer* parameters (Breakfast 77.2 vs 75.0; 50Salads 83.9 vs 79.6; 1.10M vs 1.13M).
**Relevance:** it is the only method here that *directly optimises the quantity our business metric measures* (the segment-label sequence), and it is explicitly designed for "long input sequences opposed to short output sequences and **relatively few videos**". **Two negatives:** its authors warn Transformers "are very data-hungry" (consistent with our Transformer result), and the repo is **AGPL-3.0 and archived** — a real licensing hazard for an industrial deployment.

### 4.3 DiffAct / DiffAct++ (ICCV 2023 / TPAMI 2025) — [arXiv 2303.17959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17959)
The diffusion process runs on the **label sequence itself** (`Y0 ∈ {0,1}^{L×C}` from the one-hot ground truth; `Ys = √ā_s·Y0 + ε√(1−ā_s)`), with video features used **only as conditioning**. The "unified masking strategy" masks the **features** in four ways — `M^N` none, `M^P` all-zero (position prior), `M^B` drop frames near GT boundaries (boundary ambiguity), `M^R` blank all frames of a random action class (**relational dependency**) — and is used in training only, never at inference. S = 1000 training steps, 25 inference steps. Paper's own Table 1/7: 50Salads Edit 85.0 / F1@50 83.7 / Acc 88.9; Breakfast 78.4 / 64.6 / 76.4; GTEA Edit 89.6 / F1@50 84.7 / Acc 82.2; **0.975M params, 4.96–10.85 GFLOPs, 2.09 s at 25 steps**; I3D 2048; code `Finspire13/DiffAct`, **MIT**. **Its own stated limitation: the advantage is "not as significant" on the small GTEA dataset** — directly relevant to us. **DiffAct++** (IEEE TPAMI 47(3):1644–1659, 2025) adds *consistency gradient guidance* and extends evaluation to Assembly101, but the body is paywalled (IEEE serves HTTP 202/empty), there is no arXiv preprint, and no separate code repo — so **no metrics or parameter counts for DiffAct++ could be verified**.
**Relevance:** this is the **only method in the survey that models the label sequence generatively** — the most conceptually aligned mechanism with "whole-segment identity confusion", because the object being denoised *is* the segment-label sequence. **Cost:** 1000 diffusion training steps and an iterative inference loop over full sequences make it the most expensive option here, and its gains come from a learned conditional model — exactly the part most at risk with tens of thousands of frames. Note also that its identity-relevant masking (`M^R`, relational dependency) perturbs *features*, not labels.

### 4.4 LTContext (ICCV 2023) — [arXiv 2308.11358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11358)
Sparse attention that reaches the **full video context**, showing full-context modelling is necessary for best performance; evaluated on 50Salads, Breakfast and **Assembly101** (industrial assembly). **0.72M params, 0.6 s inference**.
The mechanism is windowed attention (W = 64) with overlapping K/V across windows **plus** sparse long-term attention that samples 1 of every G frames from all windows (`G = 1` = full context); its central result is that **full-video context is necessary** (50 % window is worse, and the gap widens with video length). Its own headline numbers: 50Salads 89.4/87.7/82.0, Edit 83.2; Breakfast 77.6/72.6/60.1, Edit 77.0 — and it trains in 1 d 18 h versus ASFormer's 4 weeks. ⚠️ **The code is CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial)** — a hard blocker for an industrial product. I also verified the negative: **no distinct 2025 "LTContext" paper exists** (arXiv title search returns nothing; later papers only cite it as a baseline).

Its Table 2 (Assembly101, all on I3D features) is worth quoting in full, because it defines the realistic range for industrial procedural TAS: MS-TCN++ 31.6/27.8/20.6, Edit 30.7, Acc 37.1 (1.08M, 0.1 s); UVAST 32.1/28.3/20.8, Edit 31.5, Acc 37.4 (1.22M, 3.2 s); C2F-TCN 33.3/29.0/21.3, Edit 32.4, Acc 39.2 (6.89M, 0.1 s); ASFormer 33.4/29.2/21.4, Edit 30.5, Acc 38.8 (1.13M, 2.1 s); **LTContext 33.9/30.0/22.6, Edit 30.4, Acc 41.2 (0.72M, 0.6 s)**. See §6.5 for why this matters.
**Relevance is indirect.** Its premise is that long videos make full context expensive. Our videos are short (~330 frames each at our sampling rate), so full-sequence context is already free for us — we are not context-limited, and we already run offline full-sequence models. Treat LTContext as *confirmation* that an offline full-context model is the right family, not as a fix for identity confusion. It is also one of the four baselines in the new industrial-assembly benchmark IMPACT (§4.9).

### 4.5 BaFormer (NeurIPS 2024) — [arXiv 2405.15995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15995)
Reframes TAS as **per-segment classification**: "we tokenize each video segment as an instance token, endowed with intrinsic instance segmentation"; instance queries produce segment proposals, a global query predicts class-agnostic boundaries, and a **voting strategy** classifies boundary-wise segments at inference. Verified numbers: **1.63M params, 4.54 GFLOPs, 0.139 s inference, 300 epochs at batch 1** (≈6 % of DiffAct's time, 10 % of its FLOPs); 50Salads 89.3/88.4/83.9, Edit 84.2, Acc 89.5; Breakfast 79.2/74.9/63.2; GTEA 92.0/91.3/83.5. Query count M is dataset-specific (90 for GTEA, 100 elsewhere), and GT matching by **ordered-class** works best (transcript matching is poor). Code: `github.com/peiyao-w/BaFormer` — ⚠️ **no LICENSE file could be found**.
**Relevance:** high conceptually — the decision is made *once per segment*, which is precisely the granularity of our failure. **Risk:** it is an efficiency paper; its contribution is achieving competitive accuracy with dense-input-free inference, and its segments derive from learned proposals whose training needs many segments per class. We have ~219 GT segments in 2,639 test frames and only 4 non-idle classes with >20 segments — proposal learning for such small classes is where this would break.

### 4.6 FACT (CVPR 2024) — [CVF page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Lu_FACT_Frame-Action_Cross-Attention_Temporal_Modeling_for_Efficient_Action_Segmentation_CVPR_2024_paper.html)
Parallel branches: a **frame branch** (dilated conv) and an **action branch** (Transformer over a fixed number of learnable **action tokens**), with cross-attention transferring information both ways; and — the key detail for us — a **matching loss** that forces "each action token to uniquely encode an action segment". Tokens are matched one-to-one to segments (60 tokens on GTEA/Breakfast, 300 on EgoProceL).
**Reported:** SOTA on all four of its datasets. On **GTEA — which the paper itself calls "a small-scale dataset to test learning with limited data" (28 videos, 11 actions, ~33 segments/video, i.e. a per-video segment density very close to ours)** — FACT reaches **Edit 91.4 / F1 93.5,92.1,84.1 / Acc 86.1**, versus MS-TCN++ at **Edit 83.5 / F1 88.8,85.7,76.0 / Acc 80.1**. On EgoProceL (1055 videos, 130 actions): Edit **75.7** vs MS-TCN++ 62.4, ASFormer 64.9, UVAST 67.7, LTContext 61.3, DiffAct 68.4 — FACT wins by ~7 edit points over the previous best.
**Its own small-data adaptation is the most encouraging detail:** in the supplementary material FACT uses only **2 update blocks on GTEA "as it is a small-scale dataset and using fewer blocks reduces overfitting"** — i.e. the authors' own remedy for our regime is *fewer* blocks, consistent with in-house evidence and with MS-TCN++'s shared-parameter result. Code: `ZijiaLewisLu/CVPR2024-FACT`, MIT. Other verified numbers: Breakfast Edit 79.7 / F1@50 66.2 / Acc 76.2; EgoProceL 75.7/60.8/77.6; EPIC-Kitchen 22.3/18.5/20.8.
**Cost:** 1.9M params, 5.5 GFLOP (vs MS-TCN++ 1.0M/4.5) — i.e. roughly *our* parameter scale, not 10× it, and cheaper than DiffAct.
**Independent domain evidence:** a 2026 *Journal of Medical Robotics Research* paper applies FACT verbatim to **Cholec80 (7 phases), AutoLaparo (7 phases), MultiBypass140 (12 phases / 46 steps)** — DOI `10.1142/s2424905x26500029`. And **IMPACT (ACM MM 2026)** uses FACT as one of four industrial-assembly TAS baselines. So this mechanism has already been shown to transfer to exactly our class-count regime in medical and industrial settings.

### 4.7 DIR-AS, TST, BIT — the "segment-identity by design" family
* **DIR-AS** ([arXiv 2304.02110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02110)) is the most on-point *naming* of our problem: it "decouple[s] two inherent goals in action segmentation, i.e. (1) **individual identification** solved by frame-wise supervision, and (2) **temporal reasoning** tackled by **action set prediction**". GTEA Acc 82.8 / Edit 89.7; Breakfast 74.7 / 76.6. **But:** no peer-reviewed venue could be found, no code exists, and — importantly — **the paper does not report that identity errors dominate**; its own failure analysis is about under-segmentation of short segments and residual over-segmentation. Do not treat it as evidence that decoupling fixes identity confusion.
* **Temporal Segment Transformer** ([arXiv 2302.13074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13074)) denoises segment representations with segment–frame attention and models inter-segment relations. Its most useful number for us is a *control*: a variant using **only category embeddings** (i.e. per-segment label re-normalisation) already reaches Edit 79.6 on 50Salads against 82.4 for the full model. That is a cheap, high-value baseline to reproduce before building anything complex. No official code; no venue found.
* **BIT** ([arXiv 2308.14900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14900)) shares FACT's action-token construction but adds **set-prediction (Hungarian) matching**; Breakfast 80.6/75.9/64.7, Edit 79.0, and 30× faster than UVAST. Its loss ablation is the strongest single argument for token-based identity modelling: **dropping the action-token losses collapses F1@10 from 79.1 to 50.1.** Preprint only, no released code.
* **Honest negative:** no 2025–2026 TAS paper using *soft-DTW / differentiable edit-distance training losses* or *permutation-invariant set prediction* could be verified despite targeted searches (bounded by the arXiv 429 limit). If we want to optimise `edit` directly during training, there is no off-the-shelf TAS recipe to copy — but D3TW ([arXiv 1901.02598](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02598), CVPR 2019) provides **discriminative differentiable DTW** as a transferable technique.

### 4.8 C2F-TCN (TPAMI 2023) — [arXiv 2212.11078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11078)
1-D U-Net encoder–decoder whose decoder outputs are ensembled **coarse-to-fine**, plus a model-agnostic stochastic segment-max-pooling feature-augmentation trick. Reported: Breakfast Edit 68.9 / MoF 76.6; 50Salads Edit 76.3 / MoF 84.5; GTEA Edit 88.5 / MoF 81.2. ≈6M params; code `dipika-singhania/C2F-TCN` (MIT) — note the arXiv page does not link code, and the paper hardcodes a 2048→256 projection in an appendix table.
**Relevance:** the coarse-to-fine ensemble is a cheap way to gain segment-level stability and it is *already the semi-supervised backbone* used by the 2026 constrained-decoding paper. Moderate priority: it improves over-segmentation, not identity.

### 4.9 New, directly relevant work found during this survey

* **IMPACT: A Dataset for Multi-Granularity Human Procedural Action Understanding in Industrial Assembly** — [arXiv 2604.10409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10409) (ACM MM 2026): 112 trials, 13 participants, 39.5 h, five-view RGB-D, real angle-grinder assembly. Step-level TAS (26 step categories) scored with **Accuracy / Edit / F1@{10,25,50}** — our exact metric family — with LTContext, ASQuery, DiffAct and FACT as baselines on frozen I3D and VideoMAE-v2 features. **Its most useful sentence for us:** rare-phase imbalance (recovery = 2.22 % of frames) is "a major bottleneck **rather than model capacity alone**".
* **Surgical Activity Recognition with Frame-Action Cross-Attention Temporal Modeling** — *J. Medical Robotics Research* 2026, DOI `10.1142/s2424905x26500029`. FACT applied to Cholec80 / AutoLaparo / MultiBypass140; the paper's stated open problem is "consistently learning temporal dependencies at the frame-level, activity-level, and case-level across different procedure types".
* **ActFusion** — NeurIPS 2024, DOI `10.52202/079017-2855` — a second, unified diffusion model for segmentation *and* anticipation.
* **Timestamp Query Transformer** — WACV 2026, DOI `10.1109/wacv61042.2026.00487`.
* **"Hazard-Aware Duration Prior and F1-Adaptive Loss for Temporal Action Segmentation"** — 2026, DOI `10.5220/0014396200004084` (found via Crossref; **abstract not read — treat as a lead, not a verified finding**). Both halves are directly on our metric profile: an explicit duration prior (cf. Kuehne et al.'s +4.4 MoF from a length prior, §4.10) and a loss adapted to F1@k. Worth a look before designing a custom sequence-level loss.
* **ASQuery: A Query-based Model for Action Segmentation** — ICME 2024, DOI `10.1109/icme57554.2024.10687535` (Gan et al.); used as the "query-based decoding" design axis in IMPACT.
* **"Do We Really Need Temporal Convolutions in Action Segmentation?"** — ICME 2023, DOI `10.1109/icme55011.2023.00178`. ⚠️ Despite the common assumption, **it is not MLP-based** (zero MLP/Mixer mentions): it is a Temporal U-Transformer with local attention + temporal sampling. Its citable small-data result: *"Full attention fails regardless of the architecture, showing that training on small data requires sparser attention patterns"* — full attention collapses to F1@10 **4.6**, Acc 62.8. It targets boundaries, not identity.

### 4.10 Low-dimensional features and small datasets: what precedent actually exists

Two things are true at once, and the second is more useful than the first.

**(i) Mainstream TAS is an I3D/foundation-feature field.** Every benchmark in this survey (GTEA, 50Salads, Breakfast, Assembly101, EgoProceL, EPIC-Kitchen) feeds the temporal model 2048-d I3D features or foundation-model features (ActionCLIP, DINOv2 in VidParse). So the field's *hyperparameters* — widths, token counts, attention windows, stage counts — are tuned for rich, highly redundant input, and our 144-dim ROI histogram is off-distribution for every recipe we might copy.

**(ii) But low-dimensional input is not a handicap in the sub-literature that actually uses it — and *motion content* in the representation is what matters.**
* **MS-TCN, Table 10 (Breakfast) — the one controlled feature-type swap in this survey, same architecture, only the input changed: IDT F1@{10,25,50} = 58.2/52.9/40.8 vs I3D 52.6/48.1/37.9** (Edit 61.4 vs 61.7, Acc 65.1 vs 66.3). The paper's own words: *"the impact of the features is very small… the model achieves a better F1 score when using the IDT features."* **"We must move to I3D" is not supported by the survey evidence — the single controlled test argues mildly against it.**
* **The TCN line's genuine low-dim precedent is sensor data, not hand-crafted video features.** In the TCN workshop paper the 50 Salads accelerometer stream is **7 devices × 3 axes = 21 channels**, and the TCN reaches **Edit 65.6 / Acc 82.0**, beating LC-SC-CRF (50.2/77.8) and LSTM (54.5/73.3). On **JIGSAWS kinematics** the same TCN reaches **Edit 85.8**. A *21-dimensional* input is not a ceiling in itself.
* **Funke et al., MICCAI 2019 (JIGSAWS) is the most actionable result I have found for us:** going from a *spatially-only* per-frame representation to a *spatio-temporal* one leaves **frame accuracy identical (79.9 → 79.9)** while **Edit jumps 41.4 → 64.0 and F1@10 55.4 → 75.2** (Table 2). Our numbers (Edit 51.08, F1@0.1 40.01) sit in the *spatially-only* band — and our 144-dim vector is **instantaneous**: `[presence, count, max_area]` per (class, ROI) contains no velocity, no derivative, no motion term anywhere.
* **MS-TCRNet** (52 training sequences, **36-dim** input, unchanged): MS-TCN++ Edit **45.8** → G-MS-TCRNet Edit **75.8**, F1@50 37.9 → 57.6 — a ~30-point Edit swing at our data scale by changing the *temporal model*, not the features. On JIGSAWS its **14-dim kinematics-only** model beats every video-only baseline (Acc 86.4 / Edit 90.5 vs TDRN 84.6 / 90.2).
* **A controlled 14-encoder sweep settles the "does the feature extractor matter?" question — and says no.** *Exploring Vision-Language Models for Open-Vocabulary Zero-Shot Action Segmentation* ([arXiv 2602.21406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21406), **ICRA 2026**, verified from the `Comments` field) holds the pipeline fixed and swaps the encoder across **14 VLMs in 4 families spanning 149.62M → 2419.27M parameters (16.2×)**. I read Tables V–VII myself; the numbers are unambiguous:
  * **Encoder choice moves the average metric (mean of F1@{10,25,50}, Edit, Acc) by only 2.2–8.9 points** — 50Salads 34.9→41.7 (spread 6.8), GTEA 15.0→23.9 (8.9), Breakfast 44.4→46.6 (**2.2**).
  * **Removing one pipeline stage costs 15.8–40.6 points of the same average** (50Salads ↓31.4/↓38.8; GTEA ↓15.8/↓20.5; Breakfast ↓32.8/↓40.6).
  * So **what you do with the features is worth ≈1.8–18× which features you use** — at identical feature quality, the pipeline design dominates.
  * And **scaling up does not help**: *"simply scaling VLMs up does not yield better action-segmentation performance… **Larger checkpoints underperform their smaller counterparts**"*, in **all four** VLM families. The **smallest encoder in the study (149.62M) wins Breakfast**.
  **This is the same null result we already have on the model side, now reproduced on the input side by an unrelated group.** Caveat (important): that sweep is **zero-shot and training-free**, so it measures a *pipeline-stage* spread, not a spread over trained TAS backbones — it does **not** refute the trained-architecture gains in §6.3 (+16.7 Edit MS-TCN→DiffAct). It settles the *encoder* question specifically.
* **Upgrading the feature extractor can actively HURT.** LTContext (ICCV 2023) swapped features for CLIP features on 50Salads and lost **−19.0 Edit**. This corroborates, at the literature level, our own mixed result when we bolted on a 576-dim MobileNet image-embedding channel (small segment-level gain, frame-level macro-F1 down, `air_injection` recall collapsing).
* **MS-TCN's own words, verbatim:** *"As shown in Table 10, **the impact of the features is very small.** While the frame-wise accuracy and edit distance are slightly better using the I3D features, the model achieves a better F1 score when using the IDT features compared to I3D… **Additional appearance features therefore do not help in recognizing the activity.**"* (Verified directly in the primary source.) Independently corroborated by Souri et al. (arXiv 2005.09743).
* **Kuehne, Richard & Gall (IEEE TPAMI, arXiv 1906.01028) — the strongest low-dim + small-label precedent, on a 64-dim hand-crafted IDT/Fisher-vector feature:** labelling **0.25 % of frames** lifts Breakfast MoF 36.7 → **56.0**, while labelling 400× more frames adds only +5.3 more; giving the model **oracle action lengths** lifts 33.3 → **51.3**; and an explicit **length prior** adds +4.4 MoF. Two lessons for us: (i) a 64-dim hand-crafted feature is enough to reach ~56 MoF on Breakfast, and (ii) **explicit duration modelling pays** — which is exactly what constraint-aware Viterbi's duration bounds and UVAST's alignment decoder provide.
* **MS-GCN** (arXiv 2202.01727) is **not** a JIGSAWS paper (contrary to a common assumption): it uses PKU-MMD v2, HuGaDB, LARa, TUG with **54–150-dim** inputs. On **LARa** (warehouse, the closest analogue to us) it reports **F1@50 43.6 / Acc 65.6 over 113 test trials** — i.e. segmental F1@50 in the low 40s on low-dim input is a *published operating point*, not a symptom of breakage.
* **Segment *density* is a difficulty axis in its own right, and we sit in the hard part of it.** From the same paper's Tables III and X: **GTEA averages 36.3 GT segments/video with a mean segment duration of 1.94 s and scores worst (Avg 23.9)**, while **Breakfast averages 5.4 segments/video, 20.95 s per segment, and scores best (46.6)**; 50 Salads (20.7 segments, 18.59 s) sits between. Their conclusion: *"the number of fine-grained action boundaries strongly influences performance, with dense sequences of short actions being particularly challenging."* **Our test split is ~27 GT segments/video (219 segments over 8 videos) with a median segment length of 26 frames ≈ 3.5 s** — i.e. between GTEA and 50Salads, firmly in the dense/short-segment regime, and closer to the hardest benchmark than to the easiest. A verified, **non-architectural** explanation for why our setting is hard, and a reason to report density alongside any benchmark claim.
* **M2R2** (arXiv 2504.18662) ablation: **proprioception-only F1@50 74.5 vs vision-only 21.6** — another case where the low-dimensional modality wins outright.

**Provenance and caveats.** The MS-TCN Table 10 swap, the 21-channel accelerometer result, the Funke MICCAI 2019 edit jump, MS-TCRNet and the M2R2 ablation were verified from primary sources by a delegated worker whose raw artifacts are in `.tas_survey_tmp/raw/lowdim/` and notes in `.tas_survey_tmp/notes/lowdim-smalldata.md`. Flagged by that worker and repeated here: **venues for TricorNet, MS-GCN, few-shot skeleton TAS and M2R2 are UNVERIFIED** (no arXiv `Comments`/`Journal-ref` field), and one IKEA-ASM table (Aganian et al., IJCNN 2023) has cell alignment only *partially* verified. Also corrected against the brief: the TCN papers contain **zero** occurrences of "Fisher" — the widely repeated "64-dim Fisher vector TCN" story is not supported by either TCN paper; the MERL Shopping setup is **PCA to 50 components** over stacked CNN features.

**What this implies for us, concretely.** The literature's cheapest untried lever in our direction is not a new architecture and not a new feature contract: it is **giving the instantaneous 144-dim vector temporal content** — per-ROI first differences / short-window derivatives of `count` and `max_area` (and transition flags for `presence`) — which is exactly the spatially-only → spatio-temporal move that produced Edit 41.4 → 64.0 on JIGSAWS. This is testable in the existing pipeline without touching the model, and it is the experiment I would run before any retraining. One honest caveat from our own probes: `presence` and `count` are low-cardinality, so their derivatives are near-binary and may add little; `max_area` and `count` deltas are where the signal would have to come from, and our §6.2 direction probe suggests derivatives will *not* rescue insert-vs-withdraw specifically.

## 5. Ranked shortlist (top 5) for *our* setting

Ranking criteria, in order: (1) does the mechanism attack *whole-segment identity* rather than boundaries; (2) can it ingest 144-dim features; (3) small-data/overfitting risk; (4) implementation cost. Where published gains and our constraints conflict, I say so.

---

### #1 — S-NCM: per-segment nearest-class-mean re-labelling *(Cost-Sensitive Learning for Long-Tailed TAS, [arXiv 2503.18358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18358); venue per arXiv `Comments` "BMCV 2024" — not Crossref-confirmed)*

**Mechanism (verified verbatim):** "we first leverage the classifier's predictions ŷ to detect segment boundaries b and then utilize frame-wise NCM predictions v̂ for labelling each segment through major voting… The segment label is determined by selecting the most frequent prediction (mode) from all frame-wise NCM predictions."

* **(a) Identity, not boundaries:** it is the purest possible attack on whole-segment identity — it *takes our boundaries as given* (which our evidence says are roughly right) and re-decides each segment's label from class-mean distances in representation space, bypassing the biased classifier. Frame-level NCM alone "disregard[s] temporal continuity and lead[s] to over-segmentation"; the segment-level majority vote is what fixes it.
* **(b) 144-dim:** yes — it needs only the model's penultimate **frame representations** (for us: mstcn2's 128-dim hidden features) and its boundary predictions. No input-dimension assumption at all.
* **(c) Small-data risk:** **the lowest of any candidate.** No retraining, no new parameters, no new loss to tune. The real risk is statistical: class means estimated from very few segments per class (`sbc` has 18 GT segments, `flush` 21). Mitigations are cheap (shrinkage toward the classifier's logits; leave-one-video-out means) and testable in an afternoon.
* **(d) Implementation cost:** **very low.** Reuse existing predictions + an existing forward pass to dump embeddings; boundaries come from our existing argmax transitions. A few hours, evaluated with the existing `val_edit` selection and the existing segment-level probes.
* **Evidence strength:** S-NCM gives deltas of up to **+8.1 F1@10 / +8.1 F1@25 / +3.7 Edit (MS-TCN, Breakfast)** in the paper's Table 2, while every competing long-tail method in the same table (CB, LA, Focal, τ-norm) gives ≈0 or negative. *Caveat on precision:* I read the mechanism sentence and this delta row verbatim from the paper's HTML, but the table reports grouped head/tail aggregates whose exact grouping semantics I inferred from its structure — treat the exact deltas as indicative, and read the table yourself before quoting them. That pattern — *representation is fine, classifier is biased* — is exactly the hypothesis most worth testing against our four bad classes.
* **Honest caveat:** this cannot invent information. If the 144-dim features genuinely do not separate a class (our §6.2 probe says they do not for insert/withdraw), NCM will just pick the wrong mean more confidently.

---

### #2 — Constraint-Aware Viterbi decoding *(arXiv 2605.10149, May 2026)*

**Mechanism:** statistical structural priors — **transition confidence, valid start/end action sets, and per-class duration bounds normalised to video length** — extracted from annotated data and enforced inside a modified Viterbi decoder. Training-free, model-agnostic, complexity **O(C·T)**.

* **(a) Identity/structure:** it does not re-label segments, but it *forbids* the structural symptoms of identity confusion: illegal transitions between semantically unrelated actions and unstable segment lengths. Its stated aim is "preventing invalid transitions and unstable segment lengths".
* **(b) 144-dim:** trivially yes — it consumes only a frame×class probability matrix.
* **(c) Small-data risk:** constraints are estimated from annotations, and we have few segments per class; duration bounds for `sbc` (median ~6.5 frames) will be noisy. But the method is *applied* not trained, so failure mode is "no gain", not "new overfitting". Also the whole point is that it needs no retraining.
* **(d) Implementation cost:** **low.** The announced repo `LUNAProject22/CAD` **404s (verified via GitHub API today)**, so this must be reimplemented — but a constrained Viterbi over our `T×C` logits with a transition mask and duration limits is ~100 lines of NumPy, and our sequences are only a few hundred frames.
* **Evidence:** Breakfast with ASFormer backbone: F1@{10,25,50} 74.1/68.7/55.5 → **78.8/73.1/58.0**, Edit 72.8 → **77.9**, Acc 72.4 → 74.6. With FACT backbone: Edit 79.7 → 81.4. 50Salads semi-supervised (ICC backbone): Edit 45.9 → **48.9**, F1@10 51.8 → 55.8. The **ablation is the key number**: plain Viterbi on logits+transitions gives Edit 39.2, constrained gives **48.9** — i.e. most of the gain comes from the *constraints*, not from decoding per se. Inference cost 2.81 s vs 148.9 s for the grammar parser (KARI) on 20 Breakfast videos.
* **Honest caveats (two, both important):** (i) on fully-supervised Breakfast its Edit (77.9) is slightly *below* KARI's 78.3 — gains are real but not uniformly dominant; (ii) **a naive transition prior alone does not work and can hurt** — verified twice, once in this paper (plain Viterbi over logits+transitions = 39.2 Edit vs 48.9 with hard constraints) and once in the broader adversarial review. Do not ship "softmax + argmax + a transition matrix"; the value is in the *hard* constraints (valid start/end, duration bounds), and hard beats soft (77.9 vs 74.6).

---

### #3 — KARI: activity-grammar parsing *(NeurIPS 2023, [arXiv 2312.04266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04266), MIT code `gongda0e/KARI`)*

**Mechanism:** induces a **context-free (PCFG) grammar** — key actions plus recursive AND/OR rules — from training action sequences, then a **generalised (Breadth-first Earley) parser** transforms frame-level probability distributions into a **grammar-consistent action sequence**; Viterbi assigns durations.

* **(a) Identity/structure:** this is the strongest published expression of "constrain which ordered *label sequences* are admissible" — the segment label sequence itself is rewritten, which is exactly the object our `edit` metric measures.
* **(b) 144-dim:** yes — consumes only the frame×class probability matrix. Zero trained parameters, no retraining.
* **(c) Small-data risk:** **the main worry.** Grammar induction needs enough training transcripts to estimate rules; with 8 test videos and few sequences per action, an under-determined grammar is possible, and the paper shows grammar quality is *critical*: a bad grammar (ADIOS) crashes ASFormer from Edit 76.5 → **58.3**. Key-action count `N_key` is sensitive. Mitigation: our workflows are highly ordered (a fixed assembly/operation procedure), so a *hand-specified* grammar or a fixed transition graph is defensible and cheap; also, the paper's own failure mode means this is easy to detect in a pilot.
* **(d) Implementation cost:** **moderate** — the parser is real work, but code exists (MIT). Budget a few days including grammar inspection.
* **Evidence:** 50Salads ASFormer **+3.4 Edit / +1.6–2.6 F1@k** (frame accuracy −0.8); MS-TCN **+4.3 Edit / +5.1–7.9 F1** — gains land on segment metrics with accuracy flat, exactly the signature we want.

---

### #4 — FACT: frame–action cross-attention with action-token matching *(CVPR 2024)*

**Mechanism:** parallel frame branch + action branch of learnable **action tokens**, bidirectional cross-attention, and a **matching loss that forces each action token to uniquely encode one action segment**.

* **(a) Identity:** **yes, by construction** — this is the only *architecture* in the list whose training objective explicitly binds a learned token to a whole segment's identity. Its own ablation (BIT's, same construction) shows that removing the action-token losses collapses F1@10 from 79.1 to 50.1, i.e. the identity mechanism carries most of the model's value.
* **(b) 144-dim:** yes. The frame branch is convolutions over the projected feature sequence; nothing assumes 2048.
* **(c) Small-data risk:** **moderate and well-characterised.** FACT's GTEA result — a dataset it explicitly calls small (28 videos, 11 actions, ~33 segments/video, close to our segment density) — is its strongest evidence: Edit **91.4** vs MS-TCN++ 83.5. But tokens are matched one-to-one to GT segments, and one-to-one matching has a **token-budget floor**: the paper switches to one-to-many matching on the densely segmented EPIC-Kitchen because "learning one-to-one matching requires at least 1500 tokens". With 4 non-idle classes holding only 18–33 total GT segments, token budget and matching supervision are the specific things to watch.
* **(d) Implementation cost:** **highest of the ranked five** — a real retrain with a new architecture, matching loss and token count to tune. But at 1.9M params / 5.5 GFLOP it is *smaller than our current 3.3M mstcn2*, so compute is not the barrier; engineering and tuning time is.
* **Extra credibility:** independently validated on 7–12-class **surgical** phase recognition (JMRR 2026) and used as an **industrial-assembly** baseline in IMPACT (ACM MM 2026). MIT-licensed code, and (unlike LTContext) no non-commercial restriction.

---

### #5 — VidParse: micro-prototype segment labelling + graph-constrained beam search *(ECCV 2026, [arXiv 2608.27562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27562), MIT license)*

**Mechanism:** a training-free framework that "treats activity understanding as a **graph-constrained inference problem**": semantic transitions are found from a temporal similarity matrix over manipulation-anchored features, segments are labelled by nearest-neighbour against **temporal micro-prototypes**, and a **beam search decoder** with **infinite cost on illegal transitions** produces the final sequence.

* **(a) Identity:** **yes — the most direct identity mechanism in this survey**, deciding the label *once per segment* from prototypes, and additionally enforcing structural legality of the sequence.
* **(b) 144-dim:** the *architecture* is feature-agnostic, but the published pipeline uses frozen foundation-model (DINOv2-class) features. With our 144-dim vectors, the micro-prototype component is likely to degrade toward plain NCM (#1) — which is why this ranks below the simpler, cheaper version of the same idea.
* **(c) Small-data risk:** no training at all, so no overfitting risk; but prototypes and similarity matrices estimated from 8 videos are fragile. Reported strength is on GTEA-scale data (Edit 87.4 vs 71.81 for ProTAS, with the comparison designed to isolate the decoder), which is encouraging at our scale.
* **(d) Implementation cost:** **moderate**, training-free (reported ~33 s CPU). Mainly a matter of re-implementing feature extraction/prototype handling for our features.
* **Why not higher:** for us, its two transferable components are *the same two ideas* as #1 and #2 (per-segment prototype labelling + hard transition constraints). If #1 and #2 show no gains on our data, VidParse's extra machinery is unlikely to create signal that isn't in the features.
* **The one substitution to consider:** if **UVAST's AGPL-3.0 licensing** can be resolved (or the mechanism re-implemented cleanly from the paper), it plausibly belongs in this slot ahead of VidParse — it is the method whose *output formulation* is designed against our exact failure mode ("high frame-wise accuracy but low discrimination performance, i.e. over-segmentation with low Edit distance"), and it achieves better Edit than ASFormer with fewer parameters. It is excluded here only on licensing grounds, not on merit.

## 6. What evidence says this will NOT help *(adversarial section — the honest half)*

This section is deliberately confrontational. The strongest evidence that an architecture search will not break our ceiling is **our own**, and it is stronger than anything in the literature, because it was measured on our data.

### 6.1 Our own probes already falsify the capacity/architecture answer
* **Capacity and architecture buy boundaries, not identity.** In the segment-level decomposition (`docs/FEATURE_STRATEGY_COMPARE.md` §19.3), scaling from `mstcn` h32 (584 GT segments) to `mstcn2` s4l10 h128 (219 GT segments) improved median boundary offsets by ~30 % (32/36 → 22/25.5 frames) while the share of GT segments whose majority-predicted label is wrong stayed flat: **49.1 % → 51.6 %**, and IoU@0.5 match rate only moved 11.0 % → 11.9 %.
* **The errors are not at boundaries.** §21: only **21.9 %** of errors lie within ≤3 frames of a label switch; **78.1 % occur ≥4 frames from any boundary**. Boundary-frame error rates (57–62 %) barely respond to capacity, while intra-segment error rates do (38.2 % → 29.7 %).
* **Capacity counter-evidence from the literature too:** UVAST gets better Edit than ASFormer (Breakfast 77.2 vs 75.0) with *fewer* parameters (1.10M vs 1.13M) — the difference is the **output formulation** (seq2seq over action segments), not scale.
* **Matched segment-level predictions are almost entirely `idle`** (92 % of `mstcn` matches, 81 % of `mstcn2` matches) — the non-idle segment identity is essentially never recovered at the right IoU.
* **The feature axis is exhausted in-house.** `docs/experiments/EXPERIMENT_REPORT_FEATURE_ACCURACY_20260923.md`: of 13 levers tested, only 4 worked (`mstcn2`; h128; the `roi-grid-144` contract; `best_metric=val_edit`), and it states flatly that **no alternative feature contract beats `roi-144`**. Adding a 576-dim MobileNet-v3 image-embedding channel gave a mixed result — small stable segment-level gains on 3/3 seeds but clearly worse frame-level macro-F1 and `air_injection` recall collapsing to zero in 2/3 seeds (`EXPERIMENT_REPORT_IMAGE_EMBED_E1_20260911.md`). TimesFM was probed and rejected (`EXPERIMENT_REPORT_TIMESFM_FEASIBILITY_20260924.md`).

### 6.2 For the worst classes, the information is provably not in the features
`docs/FEATURE_STRATEGY_COMPARE.md` §10.2 ran a direct **insert-vs-withdraw discrimination probe** across 5 feature contracts × 4 context lengths. Train AUC reaches **0.95–1.000** (with 7,056-dim centred-24 features against ~1.7 k training frames — that is memorisation), while **val AUC 0.245–0.485 and test AUC 0.32–0.57**. Lengthening context from 1 to 49 frames does not help. The conclusion in-house is that the geometry encodes "which video/which mirror", not "is the brush going in or out".
§11.3 states the three root causes plainly: `flush` — **label/visual-convention problem** (audit the timeline); `long_brush_insert / withdraw` — **information does not exist** in the features; `short_brush_cleaning` — **detection coverage is 0 % on test**, i.e. the entity is never detected.
**No offline TAS architecture can fix a category whose defining evidence is absent from the input.** For those classes, the ranked list above is expected to deliver zero. That is not a reason to skip the list — it is a reason to (i) confirm *which* errors are information-limited before spending on architecture, and (ii) report identity metrics broken out per class so that gains elsewhere are not hidden by three unfixable classes.

### 6.3 The literature says the same thing, from three independent directions
1. **Whole-class identity failure is documented and it is rare-class-driven.** G-TLA (ECCV 2024) reports that "ASFormer [and] DiffAct have **zero accuracy on 5 and 4 of 48 classes**" — zero frame accuracy means the model never emits that label at all. **IMPACT** (ACM MM 2026) concludes that on industrial assembly, rare-phase imbalance is "a major bottleneck **rather than model capacity alone**" (recovery spans 2.22 % of frames; F1 near zero for all models including DiffAct).
2. **Tail classes are where identity collapses, and the numbers are brutal.** CSL (arXiv 2503.18358) reports that "AsFormer and DiffAct exhibit **zero accuracy on 5 and 4 out of 48 actions**" on Breakfast, tail-class accuracy of **4.7–5.7 %** on Assembly101, and that global metrics "obscure the performance of tail actions". It also gives a directly relevant *context* effect for identity: the action `take_eggs` is "more easily detected when preceded by `pour_oil` compared to `take_bowl`" — i.e. **segment identity is partly a function of the surrounding sequence**, which is precisely the structure that grammar/transition-constrained decoding and S-NCM exploit. This is the strongest literature support for breaking out per-class identity metrics rather than trusting global averages.
3. **Both ends of the capacity axis have now been ablated to null.** On the model side, our own study (and MS-TCN++'s shared-vs-full GTEA result) shows capacity isn't the lever. On the **input** side, the ICRA 2026 14-encoder sweep shows a 16.2× parameter range moves segmentation quality by 2.2–8.9 points while a single pipeline stage is worth 15.8–40.6. Together: **neither a bigger model nor a bigger encoder is the answer; the pipeline/objective is.**
4. **When identity is fixed in the literature, it is fixed by *deciding differently*, not by *being bigger*.** S-NCM recovers up to +8.1 F1@25 on tail classes with a *post-hoc* nearest-class-mean vote where every parameter-level long-tail loss (CB, LA, Focal, τ-norm) gives ≈0. CAD's ablation shows constrained Viterbi 48.9 vs plain Viterbi 39.2. KARI moves Edit without moving parameters (0 trained params). The signal is consistent: **the bottleneck moves from "compute" to "decision rule"**.
5. **"Segment-level loss" is not automatically an identity fix.** The 2026 CDF segment-shape regularization turns out to be **identity-blind** (no cross-class term; it shapes the already-chosen class's probability mass). Anyone who assumes "our loss is frame-wise, so a segment-level loss will fix segment confusion" should read that equation first.

### 6.4 The literature also says don't expect a data-hungry architecture to save a small dataset
* **ASFormer's own admission** that the problem is "the lack of inductive biases with **small training sets**", and its Table 3 showing an MLP+positional-encoding variant loses **25.5 F1@10 / 23.3 Edit** — this is the quantified explanation of our own Transformer result (−24 edit).
* **ICME 2023** ("Do We Really Need Temporal Convolutions…"): *"**Full attention fails regardless of the architecture, showing that training on small data requires sparser attention patterns**"* — full attention → F1@10 **4.6**, Acc 62.8.
* **UVAST's authors**: Transformers "are very data-hungry".
* **MS-TCN++'s own small-data finding**: its *shared*, 0.66M-parameter refinement configuration **beats** its 0.99M configuration on GTEA (Edit 82.6 vs 82.0), which the authors credit to reduced overfitting "especially for small datasets like GTEA" — and it reports that a 5th stage and DDL-in-refinement-stages both degrade from overfitting. **Our 3.3M at hidden 128 is 3–5× larger than any published MS-TCN++ configuration (0.66–1.13M, 64 filters).** That is an unexamined variable, and the direction the literature suggests is *smaller and more constrained*, not wider — consistent with our own h256 result being a net loss.
* **The TPAMI survey** (*Temporal Action Segmentation: An Analysis of Modern Techniques*, TPAMI 2024, DOI `10.1109/tpami.2023.3327284`) states that sequence-based losses "such as edit scores that penalize segment-wise mistakes" are **"under-explored"**, while Gaussian smoothing is "highly effective in raising segmentation metrics" and explicit length modelling is "necessary". Translation: **the literature does not contain a validated architecture for segment-wise identity; it contains validated *post-processing and structure* recipes.**

### 6.5 Our numbers vs the industrial state of the art — a reframing
On **Assembly101** (4321 videos, 202 coarse action classes, long 7–25 min assembly videos, I3D features), LTContext's own Table 2 reports the *entire* state of the art in the **Acc 37–41 / Edit 30–33** band: MS-TCN++ Acc 37.1/Edit 30.7, UVAST 37.4/31.5, C2F-TCN 39.2/32.4, ASFormer 38.8/30.5, LTContext 41.2/30.4. Our `mstcn2` on 9 classes + idle reaches **Acc 53.98 / Edit 51.08**.
**This is not a like-for-like comparison** — Assembly101 has 202 classes to our 10, and larger label spaces depress frame accuracy mechanically — so it must not be read as "we beat SOTA". But it refutes an internal narrative worth correcting: frame accuracy in the **40–55 % band is what industrial procedural TAS looks like in the published literature**, even with 2048-d I3D features and models at 1–7M parameters. Our result is not the signature of a broken or under-sized model; it is the signature of a hard task, and it is consistent with the in-house finding that capacity is not the lever. Corroborating: IMPACT (ACM MM 2026) reports that on real industrial assembly, rare-phase recovery F1 stays "at or near zero on the left hand across all models", and concludes that imbalance is "a major bottleneck rather than model capacity alone".

### 6.6 Two further cautions, one of them about our own measurements
* **Feature backbone mismatch is real but not absolute.** All surveyed benchmarks use I3D (2048-d), VideoMAE-v2 or DINOv2 features. **However**, MS-TCN's original Breakfast results show hand-crafted **IDT features beating I3D on F1 (58.2 vs 52.6)** with Edit essentially tied (61.4 vs 61.7). So low-dimensional non-deep features are not disqualifying *in principle* — they are disqualifying *for the specific classes where our own probe shows zero discriminability*.
* **Our `edit 51.08` may not be comparable to published numbers.** Two concrete checks are worth doing before any comparison table is built: (i) is our idle/background class literally named `background` in the evaluator (the official implementations drop background by exact string match)? (ii) does our evaluator use the `max(|P|,|Y|)` normaliser, which penalises over-segmentation? If either differs, published edit scores are not a reference point for us at all. Also note `insert recall` is a metric no core TAS paper reports.
* **Measurement noise in this literature is probably larger than the deltas you will chase.** Souri et al. (arXiv 2005.09743) report, over 5 runs on Breakfast, **σ ≈ 1–2.5 with a max−min spread of 6.3 within a single method**; and the Activity Grammars paper (NeurIPS 2023) *reproduced* ASFormer on 50Salads at **F1@50 74.8 where ASFormer's own paper reports 57.4 — a 17.4-point reproduction gap**, while its own KARI contribution on that metric was +1.6. Two consequences: (a) a published "+3 to +5 Edit" for a decoding intervention is at the edge of what a single run can resolve — so **implement any shortlist item with the same multi-seed paired discipline we already use, not a single run**; (b) before benchmarking against published numbers, reproduce a known baseline in our own pipeline and compare *that*, never the paper's table.
* **Statistical fragility.** 2,639 test frames over 8 videos, with per-class GT segment counts of 18–33 for the non-idle classes, means the "51.6 % of segments wrong" figure moves in steps of ~0.5 %/segment for the smaller classes and per-class conclusions rest on very few events (`sbc`: 18 segments; `flush`: 21). Report per-class segment counts alongside any identity metric, and keep the existing multi-seed median + paired-Wilcoxon discipline.

### 6.7 Two negatives that matter, and one thing our own diagnostic apparently gets right

* **Negative #1 — do not cite Breakfast or 50Salads as "ambiguous classes".** Their own papers do *not* claim that; 50Salads explicitly describes its actions as *"unambiguous and repeatable"*. Framing our class confusion as a known property of those benchmarks would be fabrication. The ambiguity evidence that *does* exist is about **tail classes** (section 6.3) and about **label/annotation conventions in specific domains**, not about fine-grained confusion in the standard benchmarks.
* **Negative #2 — no paper could be found that reports "the fraction of ground-truth segments whose label is wrong"**, nor one that quantifies identity-error dominance over boundary error. The identity-vs-boundary decomposition in our own `tools/probe_boundary_error.py` (§6.1) is, as far as this survey can establish, **not a standard TAS diagnostic** — it is a contribution, and it is publishable. Worth knowing before we treat it as routine instrumentation.

## 8. Suggested experiment order (cheap → expensive, each with a falsification criterion)

Ordered so that the cheapest hypotheses die first, and so that architecture work is only funded once the decoding-side question is settled.

| Step | Experiment | Cost | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Metric QC:** confirm the idle class name and the `edit` normaliser match the official convention; add per-class segment counts to every identity report. | hours | — (pure hygiene) |
| 1 | **Per-class identity decomposition on the current best model**: for each class, split "wrong label" into (i) *representation-separable but classifier-wrong* vs (ii) *not separable* (use a class-mean/NCM margin on the model's own embeddings). | ~1 day | If every wrong segment is category (ii), stop — go to step 5. |
| 1b | **Give the feature vector temporal content**: add per-ROI Δcount / Δmax_area over short windows (and presence transition flags) to the 144 dims; retrain `mstcn2` unchanged. | ~1 day | No Edit/F1@k gain, or gain only via more `idle` (check non-idle frame count). This is the only feature-side experiment the literature identifies as still untried here. |
| 2 | **S-NCM** (#1) on top of existing predictions: segment-level majority vote of frame-wise NCM; also try the "category-embedding-only" control from TST (Edit 79.6 baseline). | ~1 day | No change in per-class edit/F1@k on the fixable classes. |
| 3 | **Constrained Viterbi** (#2): transition mask + valid start/end + per-class duration bounds, applied to existing logits; report the plain-Viterbi ablation (39.2 vs 48.9 pattern). | 2–4 days | Constrained ≈ plain Viterbi, or gains only by suppressing non-idle frames (check the existing "non-idle frame count / segment ratio" read-out). |
| 4 | **KARI grammar** (#3) with a *hand-specified* procedure grammar first (our workflows are ordered), then induced. | ~1 week | Bad-grammar regression (ASFormer 76.5 → 58.3 is the published failure signature) or no segment-metric gain. |
| 5 | **Label audit + detection recall for `flush` / `sbc`** — the in-house analysis already names these as label/coverage problems, and step 1 will have confirmed or refuted it. | human time | — (this is the only path that can actually fix those classes) |
| 6 | **FACT** (#4) retrain, at ≤1.9M params (not wider), with an explicit token budget sensitivity sweep. | weeks | No gain over `mstcn2` + steps 2–3; or one-to-one matching under-supervises our rare classes. |
| 7 | **VidParse** (#5) / DiffAct++ / BaFormer | weeks | Only if steps 1–4 leave identity errors that are demonstrably separable. |

**The one-line summary:** the literature's answer to whole-segment label confusion is **not more parameters** — it is (i) change what the model *outputs* so that identity is decided per segment rather than per frame (S-NCM's prototype vote, UVAST's seq2seq transcript decoder, FACT's action tokens), and (ii) forbid label sequences that cannot occur (constrained Viterbi, KARI grammar). Capacity within the current frame-wise family is a dead end, and our own evidence says that for at least three of our classes no method will help, because the evidence is simply not present in the instantaneous 144-dim vector.

**The two cheapest high-upside experiments, in order:**
1. **Add temporal content to the feature vector** (per-ROI Δcount / Δmax_area) — our vector is instantaneous, and the one study isolating this (Funke, MICCAI 2019) got **Edit 41.4 → 64.0 with frame accuracy unchanged**. One retrain, no new architecture.
2. **S-NCM then constrained Viterbi** — no retraining at all, and they target exactly the two things we know are wrong: a biased per-frame decision rule, and structurally impossible label sequences. Expect published-scale effects (±3–5 Edit) to be *at the edge of resolvability* in a single run given documented σ ≈ 1–2.5 and a 17-point reproduction gap in this literature — so run them multi-seed with our existing paired Wilcoxon discipline, or do not believe the result.

Everything else in the shortlist is a bet that the identity signal exists in our features but is being lost by the model. Step 1 of §8 is the experiment that tells us whether that bet is worth making.
